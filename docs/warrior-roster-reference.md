# Female fighter references: Samurai Warriors 4 and 5

Reviewed on 26 September 2026 through the official Koei Tecmo pages with `agent-browser`.
These two games are the primary references. Earlier games do not determine this redesign.
I inspected the character art, alternate poses, and selected gameplay screenshots on the official pages.

## Design principles

- Give each woman a different weapon outline, body outline, and movement rhythm.
- Make weapon dimensions visible from the normal game camera.
- Give blade faces thickness, metallic edges, and readable grips.
- Keep both hands attached correctly during attacks.
- Match each effect to the weapon's actual path and impact.
- Separate a calm presentation, an agile presentation, and a forceful presentation.
- Use weight shifts, footwork, torso turns, and recovery poses to establish fighting style.
- Avoid copying a named character's face, costume arrangement, palette, ornaments, or exact attack sequence.
- Keep the designs original. The following fan, ring, and sickle concepts are not claims about SW5 character equipment.

## Three original concepts

The names can remain Kaede, Ayame, and Sora. Their identities should change beyond weapon labels or colors.
All three weapons below contain broad, visible cutting surfaces. None relies on a pointed rod.

| Concept | Silhouette and personality | Weapon | Combat role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kaede, the festival duelist | High rounded hair knot, short divided coat, broad detached sleeves, visible forearms. Cheerful, theatrical, and highly competitive. | One large bladed war fan with a solid crescent cutting rim and a short grip at its base. | Wide lateral control, short range, and sweeping cuts. |

### Kaede: single bladed fan

Idle: hold the open fan beside the shoulder and keep the free hand near the hip.
Run: hold the fan low and slightly behind the body. Let the free arm balance the stride.
Attack: step diagonally, cut outward, reverse the fan across the chest, then pivot into a wide finishing sweep.
Musou: raise the fan, turn through a full circle, and finish with a broad cut and a short radial burst.
Use broad crescent trails. Avoid thrusts and the two-sword overhead pose.
Keep the fan open in combat; this avoids a separate mechanical folding rig.

### Ayame: crescent ring

Idle: hold the ring beside one thigh with the body turned slightly sideways.
Run: use short, quick steps and an active free arm. Keep the ring clear of the legs.
Attack: enter with a diagonal cut, rotate the ring plane across the chest, and finish with a rising circular cut.
Musou: make a fast curved dash, turn around the planted foot, and deliver one large circular finish.
Use one continuous ring-shaped trail. Do not use paired sword strikes.
Keep normal attacks hand-held. A thrown special requires explicit return timing and grip restoration.

### Sora: hooked sickle

Idle: hold the sickle low and outside the front knee. Keep the free hand near the centre of the body.
Run: use heavier steps and a stable torso. Let the free arm counterbalance the blade.
Attack: plant the lead foot, hook inward across the target, reverse the hips, and finish with a downward crescent cut.
Musou: brace low, draw a large rising hook, then stamp into a broad ground-level finishing arc.
Use fewer, thicker impact marks and a stronger stagger. Avoid spear thrusts or naginata-style shaft rotations.
Use a rigid sickle for the first implementation. A simulated chain would add grip, collision, and animation work.

## Practical checks

Inspect all six heroes together at the same camera angle and scale.
Identify each woman from the silhouette without relying on color.
Inspect idle, run, three attacks, and the special at quarter speed.
Check hand contact, blade direction, floor contact, and recovery timing.
Verify that each special's damage area follows its visible motion.
These concepts require separate motions and weapon geometry, not renamed male clips.
